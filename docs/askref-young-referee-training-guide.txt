PDSA Young Referee Training Guide
Real game-day questions from players, coaches, and spectators • Based on Palo Duro Soccer Association rules and IFAB Laws of the Game

Jason, I would build this as a training resource for PDSA's younger referees, especially those officiating U6, U8, U10, and U12 games.

The goal is to help them learn three things:
1. Know the correct rule.
2. Explain the decision in a short, confident sentence.
3. Handle disagreement without getting into an argument.
I reviewed the PDSA Official Rules, PDSA's parent and coaching guidance, and the IFAB 2026/27 Laws of the Game.

One important distinction: PDSA publishes its age-specific playing rules in a linked PDF, but I couldn't retrieve the contents of that PDF. Therefore, the general soccer answers below follow IFAB, while PDSA-specific requirements are identified separately. Age-specific exceptions must be checked against the PDSA document before this becomes an official training handout.
Part 1: Questions players commonly ask
These are the questions young referees are most likely to hear while play is happening.
Question	Correct answer and suggested referee response
1. "Ref, why was that a foul?"	A player tripped, pushed, held, kicked, or unfairly challenged an opponent. Say: "You tripped the player. Free kick for blue."
2. "But I got the ball first!"	Getting the ball first doesn't automatically make a challenge legal. A careless, reckless, or excessively forceful challenge can still be a foul. Say: "You got the ball, but your follow-through was a foul."
3. "That was an accident!"	A player doesn't have to intend to trip someone for a careless challenge to be a foul. Say: "I understand. It was still a careless trip."
4. "The ball hit my hand. Why is that a foul?"	Not every hand/arm contact is handball. It depends on deliberate contact or whether the arm makes the body unnaturally bigger, among other considerations. Say: "Your arm was extended and made your body bigger."
5. "Why didn't you call handball?"	Accidental contact alone doesn't necessarily mean a handball offence. Say: "The ball hit the arm, but I didn't see a handball offence."
6. "They pushed me!"	A push is a foul when it meets the standard for a careless, reckless, or excessively forceful offence. Ordinary contact alone is not necessarily a foul. Say: "I saw the contact. Keep playing."
7. "Why didn't you blow your whistle?"	The referee may allow play to continue when the team that was fouled benefits. Say: "Advantage! Keep playing!"
8. "Can I kick the ball while I'm on the ground?"	Playing while on the ground isn't automatically illegal. It becomes an offence if the player acts dangerously or commits another foul. Say: "You can play from the ground as long as it's safe."
9. "Can I use my shoulder?"	Fair shoulder-to-shoulder contact can be legal when challenging for the ball. A charge can still be careless or otherwise illegal. Say: "Fair shoulder contact is allowed. Pushing isn't."
10. "Why did you stop the game? I'm not hurt!"	The referee can stop play for a serious injury or safety concern. Say: "I stopped play to make sure you're safe."

Rules: IFAB Law 5 (Referee) and Law 12 (Fouls and Misconduct).

Part 2: Out-of-bounds, throw-ins, and restarts
Question	Correct answer and suggested response
11. "The ball touched the line. Isn't it out?"	No. The entire ball must cross the entire boundary line. Say: "The ball was still on the line. Play on."
12. "Why is that their throw-in?"	The opponents of the last player to touch the ball receive the throw-in. Say: "Blue touched it last. Red's throw."
13. "What's wrong with my throw-in?"	Under IFAB, the ball must be thrown with both hands from behind and over the head, with part of each foot on or behind the touchline. Say: "Your foot was completely inside the field."
14. "Can my foot be on the line?"	Yes. Part of each foot may be on the touchline or on the ground outside it. Say: "On the line is okay."
15. "Can I score directly from a throw-in?"	No. A throw directly into the opponent's goal results in a goal kick. Say: "No goal—goal kick."
16. "Why is it a corner instead of a goal kick?"	A defender was the last to touch the ball before it crossed their own goal line, without a goal being scored. Say: "Defender touched it last. Corner kick."
17. "Do goal kicks have to leave the penalty area?"	No. Under IFAB, the ball is in play once kicked and clearly moving. Say: "The ball is in play."
18. "Do I have to wait for the whistle?"	Not for every restart. Some free kicks require the referee's whistle; a penalty kick does. Say: "Wait for my whistle," when appropriate.
19. "Can I score straight from a corner kick?"	Yes, directly against the opponents. Say: "Yes, a corner can go straight in."
20. "Why did you call that a double touch?"	A player generally cannot touch the ball a second time after taking a restart until another player has touched it, subject to specific exceptions. Say: "You touched it twice before anyone else."

Rules: IFAB Laws 9, 13, 15, 16, and 17.

Part 3: Offside questions
These are particularly important for referees assigned to divisions where PDSA applies offside.
Question	Correct answer and suggested response
21. "How was I offside?"	A player can be penalized when involved in active play after being in an offside position when a teammate played or touched the ball. Say: "You were beyond the second-last defender when your teammate played it, and then you became involved."
22. "But I wasn't offside when I received it!"	The position is generally judged when the teammate plays or touches the ball, not when the receiver controls it. Say: "It's judged when the pass was made."
23. "I was standing offside but never touched the ball!"	Merely standing offside isn't an offence. However, interfering with an opponent can be an offence without touching the ball. Say: "Position alone isn't an offence."
24. "Can I be offside on a throw-in?"	No, not when receiving the ball directly from a throw-in. Say: "No offside directly from a throw."
25. "Can I be offside on a goal kick?"	No, not when receiving the ball directly from a goal kick. Say: "No offside directly from a goal kick."
26. "Can I be offside from a corner?"	No, not when receiving the ball directly from a corner kick. Say: "No offside directly from a corner."
27. "Can I be offside in my own half?"	A player cannot be in an offside position while in their own half. Say: "You can't be in an offside position in your own half."
28. "The defender touched the ball. Doesn't that cancel offside?"	Not always. A deliberate play by a defender may reset offside, but a deflection or save generally does not. Say: "That was a deflection, not a deliberate play."

Rule: IFAB Law 11 (Offside).

Part 4: Questions coaches commonly ask
Young referees need to know the rules and how to respond respectfully without being drawn into lengthy debates.
Question from a coach	Correct answer and suggested response
29. "Ref, can I make a substitution?"	Follow the substitution procedure for that PDSA division. Say: "Coach, wait for the next permitted substitution opportunity."
30. "Why won't you let my player back in?"	The player must follow the proper re-entry procedure. Say: "I'll signal when your player can enter."
31. "Can we change goalkeepers?"	Yes, but the referee must be informed and the change must happen during a stoppage. Say: "Yes, coach. Let me know at the stoppage."
32. "Why wasn't that a penalty?"	A penalty requires a direct-free-kick offence by a defender inside their own penalty area, subject to the applicable division rules. Say: "I didn't see a foul inside the penalty area."
33. "Why was that an indirect free kick?"	Certain offences, such as dangerous play without contact or offside, result in indirect free kicks. Say: "That offence requires an indirect free kick."
34. "How much time is left?"	Give a brief, accurate estimate if appropriate. Say: "About three minutes, coach."
35. "Can you add time for the injury?"	IFAB allows additional time for delays, but PDSA's match procedures may differ. Say: "I'll follow the league's timekeeping rules."
36. "Can you explain that call?"	A short explanation at a suitable stoppage is reasonable. Say: "Yes, coach. I called a trip. Let's continue."
37. "The other team has too many players!"	Stop play when appropriate, check the player count, and apply the relevant procedure. Say: "Thank you. I'll check the numbers."
38. "Can we play with fewer players?"	The answer depends on the division's minimum-player requirements and competition rules. Say: "We'll follow PDSA's requirements for this age group."
39. "Why did you give my player a yellow card?"	Explain the specific misconduct, such as reckless play, dissent, or delaying a restart. Say: "The caution was for a reckless challenge."
40. "Can you change your decision?"	A referee can correct certain decisions before play restarts, within the limits of Law 5. Say: "I'll consult my assistant before the restart."
41. "Can I challenge that call?"	Coaches cannot require an on-field review simply by disagreeing. Say: "Coach, I've made my decision. Let's continue."

Rules: IFAB Laws 3, 5, 7, and 12. PDSA division-specific substitutions and match procedures must be verified separately.

Part 5: Questions from parents and spectators
PDSA's published Parents Guide emphasizes that many match officials are teenagers and asks spectators not to criticize officials directly. Coaches are also expected to discourage arguing with referees. 
Palo Duro Soccer Association+1

What someone might say	Recommended referee response
42. "Are you blind? That was a foul!"	Avoid an argument. Continue officiating. If necessary: "Please keep comments respectful."
43. "You need to call it both ways!"	"I'm applying the same rules to both teams."
44. "You missed an obvious handball!"	"I had a clear view and did not see a handball offence." Only say you had a clear view if you actually did.
45. "You're letting them play too rough!"	"Player safety is my priority. I'll address unsafe challenges."
46. "How old are you? You shouldn't be refereeing!"	Do not engage personally. If harassment continues, stop at an appropriate time and seek assistance from the referee coordinator or PDSA official.
47. "Why won't you explain the call to me?"	"Please direct questions through your team's coach."
48. "That player is hurt! Stop the game!"	Assess the situation. Stop immediately for a serious injury or other immediate danger. "I'm stopping for player safety."
49. "The other team's parents are yelling at our kids!"	Do not personally confront the crowd. Involve the coaches and responsible league personnel.
50. "I'm reporting you!"	"You may contact the league. Let's keep the game respectful." Do not retaliate or argue.

An important lesson for young referees
A referee does not need to answer every comment from the sidelines.
Three habits worth practicing

1. Explain a decision briefly when appropriate.
2. Never get into a shouting match with an adult.
3. Ask the referee mentor, field marshal, commissioner, or other responsible PDSA official for help when conduct becomes abusive.

Part 6: Goalkeepers and penalty kicks
Question	Correct answer and suggested response
51. "Can the goalkeeper pick up a pass from a teammate?"	Not when a teammate deliberately kicks the ball to the goalkeeper and the goalkeeper handles it. Say: "Deliberate kick to your goalkeeper. Indirect free kick."
52. "Can a goalkeeper pick up a ball thrown in by a teammate?"	Not directly from that teammate's throw-in. Say: "You can't handle a teammate's throw-in directly."
53. "Can a goalkeeper use their hands outside the box?"	A goalkeeper is subject to the same handball restrictions as any other player outside their own penalty area. Say: "Outside the penalty area, you cannot handle the ball."
54. "Why did you call a corner when the goalkeeper held the ball?"	Under IFAB's current rule, a goalkeeper who controls the ball with the hands or arms for more than eight seconds is penalized with a corner kick. Say: "The goalkeeper held it beyond the permitted time." Verify PDSA's adoption of this rule.
55. "Can players stand in front of the goalkeeper?"	Players may challenge fairly but cannot illegally impede or interfere with the goalkeeper. Say: "Give the goalkeeper room to release the ball."
56. "Does the goalkeeper have to stay on the goal line during a penalty?"	At the moment of the kick, the goalkeeper must have at least part of one foot touching, in line with, or behind the goal line. Say: "Keep one foot on or in line with the goal line until the kick."
57. "Can the penalty taker stop during the run-up?"	Feinting during the run-up is permitted, but illegal feinting once the run-up is completed is penalized. Say: "You can vary your approach, but you can't illegally fake the final kick."

Rules: IFAB Laws 12 and 14; current goalkeeper holding-time provisions.

Part 7: PDSA-specific weather and safety questions
These answers come directly from PDSA's published local rules and are especially important at the Brown Road Soccer Complex.
Question	PDSA rule and referee response
58. "Why did we stop for lightning? It's not raining!"	PDSA requires suspension when lightning occurs within 10 miles. Say: "Lightning is within the safety radius. Everyone must seek shelter."
59. "The lightning warning stopped. Can we start immediately?"	No. PDSA requires a 30-minute shelter period, restarted after each subsequent lightning strike. Say: "We must complete the 30-minute waiting period."
60. "Can we keep playing if the wind is strong?"	PDSA's weather guidelines identify sustained winds of 35 mph or gusts greater than 50 mph as suspension conditions. Say: "We need to follow the league's wind-safety policy."
61. "Why are we stopping for water breaks?"	PDSA uses heat-safety guidance based on wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT), with possible additional hydration breaks. Say: "We're taking a safety break because of the heat conditions."
62. "Why can't we play on this wet field?"	PDSA identifies standing water, snow, and fields the board considers unsuitable as reasons for suspension. Say: "The field conditions aren't safe for play."
63. "We were winning when the game stopped. Does the score count?"	PDSA generally treats weather-suspended matches differently depending on whether at least 50% of the match was completed. Exceptions can be agreed to by both coaches and the division commissioner. Say: "The league will determine the result under its suspension policy."
64. "Who decides whether a suspended match gets rescheduled?"	PDSA's published policy governs this, with potential agreement between both coaches and the division commissioner. Say: "The coaches and commissioner will handle rescheduling."

Source: PDSA Official Rules — Weather and field conditions.

Five things I would teach every young PDSA referee
Safety comes first.

Stop for serious injuries, dangerous conditions, and PDSA weather alerts.



Keep explanations short.

"Trip. Blue free kick." A young referee doesn't need to deliver a speech.



Call what you actually see.

Don't guess simply because a coach or spectator is demanding a whistle.



Respect goes both ways.

Referees should speak respectfully, and coaches and spectators are expected to do the same.



Ask for help when needed.

Use a referee mentor or league official for unfamiliar rules, confrontations, or safety problems.
